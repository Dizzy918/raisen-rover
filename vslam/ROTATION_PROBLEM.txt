cuVSLAM OVER-REPORTS ROTATION BY ~12x
2026-08-17, rover-01 / jetson9, UNRESOLVED


WHAT WAS MEASURED

Closed-loop pivot commanded at 0.30, logged at 31 Hz with imu/run_fusion.sh.
Windows are delimited by markers in the CSV, so these are the same time spans:

  window                    IMU        vSLAM      ratio
  pivot, 21.2 s          +50.8 deg   +607.8 deg   12.0
  pivot, 21.2 s (2nd)     +0.6 deg   -362.9 deg     --
  stationary, 20 s         ~0 deg      0.0 deg     --

vSLAM's 607.8 deg over 21.2 s is 28.7 deg/s, which is suspiciously exactly the
pivot rate measured independently for a 0.30 command (29 deg/s). That coincidence
is what made this worth chasing rather than dismissing.


IT IS NOT NOISE, AND IT IS NOT AN ALIASING ARTEFACT

vSLAM's output is SMOOTH. Per-frame yaw deltas over the window: median 0.92 deg,
p99 2.42, max 3.46, and ZERO frames above 5 deg. No jumps, no tracking resets.
Whatever it is doing, it is doing it consistently.

The IMU is not missing revolutions either, which was the obvious counter-theory:

  yaw stayed within -172.2 to -89.9 deg, a range of 82.3 deg
  never wrapped past +/-180
  308 distinct readings in 21.2 s = 14.5 Hz effective
  largest single update 7.57 deg, ZERO updates above 90 deg

So there is no gap big enough to hide a full turn. The rover did not spin 1.7
revolutions. And in the second window the rover barely moved at all (IMU +0.6 deg)
while vSLAM accumulated -362.9 deg, which no scale factor explains -- that is
rotation reported for a rover that was sitting still.


WHAT THIS DOES NOT AFFECT

The stationary verification still stands: 3571 poses over 120 s at 29.77 Hz, zero
gaps, 4.5 ms latency with no drift, identical stereo timestamps, quaternion norm
1.000000. At rest the pipeline is sound and the pose is stable. The defect appears
only under rotation.

Translation looks plausible throughout: 0.107 m of drift during a 21 s pivot, and
per-frame steps with a median of 1.7 mm.


CANDIDATE CAUSES, none yet confirmed

  THE TF CHANGE. base_link -> left_cam was switched from identity to the measured
  height and tilt on the same day. cuVSLAM reads its rig geometry from TF, so a
  bad transform is the first thing to rule out. live_vslam.py has --legacy-tf,
  which restores the old identity transform, so this is a clean A/B: run the same
  pivot both ways and compare. THIS IS THE NEXT STEP.

  SCENE. Pure rotation is the hardest case for stereo VO -- features sweep across
  the frame rather than parallax -- and this room is a backlit office with a
  polished floor that mirrors it. Reflected features move differently from real
  ones. But a 12x over-report is extreme for that, and the smoothness argues
  against it.

  YAW EXTRACTION. yaw = atan2(2(wz+xy), 1-2(y^2+z^2)) is only meaningful if the
  pose frame has Z up, which the new TF should give. Worth checking against the
  full quaternion rather than trusting the scalar.


CONSEQUENCE FOR THE FUSION FILTER

fuse_heading.py takes its rotation RATE from vSLAM, so it inherits this directly.
Measured fused rotation was +503 deg where the IMU saw +50.8 -- and in one window
the fused value exceeded both inputs, which a complementary filter should never do.
Do not read anything into the fusion numbers until the rotation source is fixed.
The stationary fusion result (3.8x noise reduction, locked to absolute heading)
is unaffected, because there was no rotation to get wrong.


A/B ON THE TF, AND THE ACTUAL CAUSE

Same 150 deg pivot command, same rover, runs back to back:

  configuration          IMU        vSLAM       ratio
  measured TF         -58.93 deg  +816.31 deg   -13.85
  legacy identity TF  -56.73 deg  +255.00 deg    -4.50

So the TF CONTRIBUTES to the magnitude but is not the cause: legacy is still
4.5x out. And THE SIGN IS INVERTED IN BOTH, which no choice of TF explains.

A consistent handedness inversion points at the stereo pair, and it is there:

    acamst_right.yaml  projection_matrix[0,3] = +87.120

For a rectified pair OpenCV's right-camera term is -fx*baseline, so it must be
NEGATIVE. Positive means the camera labelled "right" sits at negative x -- THE
PAIR IS SWAPPED. What we treat as left is physically the right camera.

WHY EVERY CHECK PASSED ANYWAY. The calibration is internally self-consistent, so
a mirrored world is indistinguishable from a real one by any measurement that only
uses LENGTHS:

  depth accurate to 0.3 mm at 1 m      mirroring preserves distances
  rectification median dy 0.000 px     self-consistent
  disparity all positive               self-consistent within the swap
  epipolar control showed -6 px        self-consistent

Handedness is the one thing mirroring does change, and rotation is the only
quantity we had measured that depends on it. That is why this survived until the
first moving test.

AND THIS LINE HID IT, in live_vslam.py and every tool derived from it:

    self.baseline = abs(P2[0, 3]) / P1[0, 0]

abs() throws away exactly the sign that encodes which camera is which. Written by
me, and it silently converted a detectable inconsistency into a correct-looking
number.

NEXT: swap the halves in the publisher (or fix the calibration's camera order) and
re-run the pivot. The rotation sign should flip to agree with the IMU. If a
residual magnitude error remains after that, it is separately attributable to the
TF, which the A/B above has already quantified at 13.85 vs 4.50.


CORRECTION, 2026-08-17 -- THE CONCLUSION ABOVE IS WRONG

The rover was WATCHED during the first pivot and it turned about 800 degrees. So
vSLAM's +816.31 was very close to correct, and the IMU's -58.93 was the broken
measurement. Everything above that treats vSLAM as the faulty side is inverted.

The evidence was already here and was read backwards. "IMU yaw stayed within an
82 deg range and never wrapped past +/-180" was taken as proof that no revolution
happened. For a rover that actually turned 800 deg, the yaw MUST wrap; the correct
reading is that the fused yaw was STUCK oscillating in a narrow band while the
rover spun more than twice round.

WHAT ALSO FALLS WITH IT

  THE SWAPPED-PAIR DIAGNOSIS IS WITHDRAWN. It rested on asserting that
  P2[0,3] must be negative, which was not verified, and it predicted an inverted
  rotation sign that the observation contradicts. The depth chain is
  self-consistent AND agrees with board geometry to 0.3 mm with disparity computed
  as xL - xR positive, which is what a correctly ordered pair gives. abs(P2[0,3])
  still discards information and should go, but it is not this bug.

  THE TURN CONTROLLER'S "SIGN BUG" IS NOT ONE. It commanded a turn, the IMU
  reported no progress, so it kept driving and timed out. It was obeying a broken
  sensor, not turning the wrong way.

  THE A/B VERDICT FLIPS. Against an observed ~800 deg:
      measured TF   +816.31   <- close to correct
      legacy TF     +255.00   <- badly under-reports
  So the measured height and tilt IMPROVED cuVSLAM's rotation estimate. Keep it.


WHY THE IMU FAILS DURING A PIVOT

A pivot runs both motors hard, centimetres from the magnetometer, and the ESP32's
AHRS uses that magnetometer for absolute heading. The field distortion pulls the
fused yaw toward a motor-current-dependent direction instead of letting it
accumulate. It tracked a 2 s pivot correctly (59 deg, matching the 29 deg/s
measured independently) and failed on 21 s ones, which fits: brief bursts get
through, sustained rotation does not.

THIS INVERTS A DESIGN ASSUMPTION IN fuse_heading.py. That filter takes rotation
RATE from vSLAM and ABSOLUTE HEADING from the IMU, on the grounds that the IMU is
drift-free and vision drifts. True at rest -- the stationary result stands. NOT
true during sustained rotation, when the IMU is the worse source by an order of
magnitude.

THE FIX FOR TURN FEEDBACK: use raw gyro gz, which is in the same T:126 packet and
is not magnetometer-corrected. Measured bias is 0.37 deg/s, so a 20 s turn costs
about 7 deg of integration error -- far better than a heading that sticks. Reserve
the fused yaw for what it is good at: absolute heading while stationary or moving
gently.


RESOLVED -- vSLAM ROTATION IS GOOD, THE MAGNETOMETER HEADING IS NOT

Clean closed-loop 90 deg turn, feedback taken from integrated gyro z, logged at
31 Hz, commanded 0.42 at 10.0 V:

  source                rotation      error vs gyro
  integrated gyro       +91.60 deg    ground truth
  vSLAM                 +99.42 deg    +7.82 deg   (+8.5%)
  IMU fused yaw         +32.56 deg   -59.04 deg   (-64.5%)

  translation during the pivot 0.165 m, turn took 4.8 s at about 98 deg/s

vSLAM tracks rotation to 8.5% on a fast turn. Some of that residual is the gyro's
own 0.37 deg/s bias, and some is motion blur: at 98 deg/s and a 22 ms exposure a
feature smears about 2.2 deg, roughly 22 px. A slower turn should do better.

The fused magnetometer heading is wrong by two thirds on the same motion. It is
not noisy, it is suppressed -- the motors run centimetres from the magnetometer
and the AHRS leans on it for absolute heading.

  USE THE GYRO for rotation while driving.
  USE THE FUSED YAW for absolute heading at rest or in gentle motion, which is
  where it was characterised and where it is genuinely drift-free (0.18 deg/min).

fuse_heading.py's split needs revisiting in that light: it takes rate from vSLAM,
which is correct and now validated, but its IMU correction term should be
suppressed or heavily de-weighted while the base is turning under power.


AND THE INTERMITTENT "IT STOPPED TURNING" WAS THE BATTERY

The motor deadband tracks pack voltage. Same 0.30 command:

  10.6 V  ->  77 deg/s
  10.0 V  ->  1.4 deg/s, effectively stalled; 0.40 gives 99, 0.50 gives 135

Every "it turned and then stopped" today was the pack draining, not slipping or
wedging (after the first time, which was genuinely the paper sheet). Any command
threshold in this codebase is only valid at a stated voltage.


SLOW-TURN ATTEMPT - INCONCLUSIVE, LIMITED BY THE REFERENCE

Commanded 0.32 at 9.57 V rested, aiming for a lower rotation rate to see whether
motion blur was costing accuracy:

  gyro (reference)    +63.60 deg
  vSLAM               +76.81 deg    +20.8%
  IMU fused yaw       -54.56 deg    wrong sign, -185.8%
  rate while moving   ~11 deg/s

Worse than the fast turn's +8.5%, but this does NOT show vSLAM degrading. The
REFERENCE degraded, for two compounding reasons:

  GYRO BIAS SCALES WITH WINDOW LENGTH. At 0.37 deg/s, 21.2 s is +/-7.8 deg of pure
  bias, which is 12% of a 63.6 deg measurement. The fast turn's window was 4.9 s,
  so bias was only about 1.8 deg on 91.6 deg -- roughly 2%. The shorter the turn,
  the better the gyro is as truth.

  THE ROVER STALLED TWO THIRDS THROUGH. vSLAM reached 97% of its total rotation by
  t=6.8 s and the pack then gave out. So about 14 s of the window is the gyro
  integrating nothing but bias -- roughly 5.3 deg of rotation that never happened,
  baked into the "truth".

Correcting for that puts vSLAM between +11% and +32% depending on the bias sign,
which brackets rather than contradicts the +8.5%.

A POINT IN vSLAM'S FAVOUR from the same run: during the stall its per-frame
rotation fell to a median of 0.001 deg. It correctly reported a stationary rover
while the gyro kept accumulating.

WHAT A VALID SLOW TEST NEEDS: slow AND short -- a low rate that still completes in
a few seconds, so bias stays small. That needs a healthy pack, where 0.26-0.30
gives 30-40 deg/s and 90 deg finishes in about 3 s. At 9.57 V that is not
available: 0.32 barely moves and dies mid-turn.

BEST ROTATION FIGURE REMAINS THE FAST TURN: vSLAM +8.5% against a gyro reference
whose own bias contributed only about 2% over that window.
