RUNNING THE ALGORITHM ON ONE REAL ROVER: READINESS CHECK
2026-08-18, before any hardware run


FIRST, THE THING I COULD NOT FIND

There is no single-rover logic on the algorythm branch. Three commits, and the
mission is two-rover throughout:

  sim.py          run() hardwires Rover(1, *START_A) and Rover(2, *START_B), and
                  the stage machine is STAGE_1_MARKER -> 1B_SPIN -> 2_WALL_FOLLOW
                  -> 2B_RENDEZVOUS -> 3_PARTITION_EXPLORE. Three of those five
                  stages exist only because there are two machines.
  server.py       _run_mission() imports sim and says, in its own docstring,
                  "The mission itself, on its own thread. Replace this for real
                  hardware."
  no CLI flag     neither sim.py nor server.py takes --rovers or anything like it

So either the single-rover version is not pushed, or it means "run the parts that
work for one rover". Everything below assumes the second reading. If there is
another copy somewhere, use it instead of building this.


WHAT IS ALREADY HARDWARE-READY, VERIFIED BY READING IT

The README's claim holds up: "when the hardware arrives you delete sim.py ... and
nothing in mapping.py, explore.py, merge.py or wallfollow.py changes."

  wallfollow.py   ZERO references to World. WallFollower.step(sonars, x, y, yaw,
                  dt, blocked, cam_fwd) takes {fwd,left,right} in METRES plus a
                  pose and returns a velocity. Drop-in.
  explore.py      ZERO references to World. find_frontiers(grid) and
                  island_mask(grid) need only a grid.
  mapping.py      OccupancyGrid is clean. Its three World references are all in
                  coverage scoring, not in the map itself.

And OccupancyGrid already takes BOTH of our sensors:

  integrate(origin_xy, points_xy, max_range=6.0)              stereo depth
  integrate_sonar(origin_xy, yaw, readings, mount_angles,     the three HC-SR04s
                  cone_rad=0.262, max_range=4.0)

world.py already defines mount_angles = {fwd 0, left +pi/2, right -pi/2}, which is
exactly the three channels we have. sonar3 not existing costs nothing.

NOT applicable to one rover, and all of it is in explore.py or merge.py:
split_targets, partition_geodesic, enclosed_region, filter_frontiers_by_region,
merge.fuse. Ignore rather than adapt.


WHAT HAS TO BE BUILT: A DRIVER, NOT AN ALGORITHM

Per tick, at 10 Hz:

  pose            cuVSLAM, x/y/yaw in the rover's own frame. Already running at
                  31 Hz, rotation validated to +1.9%, translation to a few percent.
  stereo points   raisen depth -> CameraToBase -> base_link, which is what
                  pipeline/run_pipeline.py's GridConsumer already produces.
  sonar           three channels, cm -> METRES, and -1 -> max_range. Their doc has
                  the right handling for a timeout: "carve free, mark nothing."
  motors          WallFollower gives (v, w) in m/s and rad/s. Convert with the
                  measured calibration below, then apply FORWARD_SIGN = -1.

Then integrate, publish a frame, repeat. The stage logic for one rover is: spin to
find a wall, follow the perimeter until the trail closes on its start, then drive
frontiers until none remain. No marker stage, no rendezvous, no partition, no merge.


FIVE THINGS THAT WILL BITE, IN ORDER OF HOW MUCH

1. THE DEADBAND IS FINE. THIS ITEM IS WITHDRAWN.

   It read: measured deadband about 0.28 m/s against a simulated 0.035, eight times
   larger, so a follower built to creep at a 15 cm standoff could not creep at all.
   That was wrong. The 0.24 command floor it rested on was itself wrong -- almost
   certainly measured in reverse, before it was known that positive L/R drives this
   rover backward.

   Measured properly against the Jetson's own rover2_obstacle_control.py, which
   cruises at command 0.10:

     0.10 -> 0.079 m/s     fit over three points: 1.238 * command - 0.0469
     0.30 -> 0.320 m/s     deadband (speed = 0) at command 0.038
     0.45 -> 0.513 m/s     residuals under 0.005 m/s

   So the chassis creeps, the simulation's V_DEADBAND = 0.035 was closer to right
   than my measurement, and at 0.079 m/s the reaction distance is 8.9 cm against a
   15 cm standoff. Wall-following is viable, with 6 cm of margin, provided it runs
   near that speed rather than at 0.30.

2. THE SONAR REFRESHES AT 2.3 Hz AND THE MISSION TICKS AT 10 Hz.

   The firmware fires the four sensors 60 ms apart on purpose so they do not hear
   each other, plus a 200 ms tail: a full cycle is about 440 ms. So four ticks in
   five see a stale reading, and at 0.32 m/s the rover covers 14 cm between updates
   -- comparable to the standoff it is trying to hold. Options: tick the mission
   at 2-3 Hz, or drive slower, or let the follower know a reading's age.

3. THE ROVER DOES NOT DRIVE STRAIGHT AND NOTHING CORRECTS IT.

   0.4% of distance at command 0.30, 1.0% at 0.45. A metre at speed lands 12 cm
   off course. The follower closes the loop on sonar so it will absorb some of
   this, but dead-reckoned segments will not.

4. V_MAX IS WRONG THE OTHER WAY. Simulated 0.45 m/s, measured about 0.58 at full
   scale (0.320 m/s at command 0.30, 0.513 at 0.45). Anything computing a command
   from a target speed will undershoot by about 20%.

5. THE STEREO NEAR LIMIT IS BETTER THAN ASSUMED. mapping.py says stereo "cannot
   see closer than 0.50 m (its disparity search has a fixed maximum)". Ours reaches
   0.389 m at numDisparities 224. So the sonar/stereo handover can sit at 0.39 m
   rather than 0.50, and there is 11 cm less blind band than the comment expects.


ONE PROBLEM THAT DISAPPEARS ENTIRELY WITH ONE ROVER

The magnetometer's positional offset -- 16 deg after 79 cm of travel, persistent,
noise unchanged either side -- broke the premise that two rovers share a frame
through magnetic north. With ONE rover there is no frame to share. Its own vSLAM
yaw is the frame, and that drifted 0.23 deg over 95 s and 0.00 deg over 72 s
stationary. So the hardest measurement problem in the two-rover design is simply
not in scope for this run. Worth knowing before anyone spends effort on it.


THE UI WILL CRASH ON A SINGLE-ROVER FRAME

viewer.html reads both rovers unguarded in at least three places:

  trailB.push(f.b)                     line 428
  drawRover(f.b, "#37d0d6", "B")       line 500
  f.b[0].toFixed(1)                    line 780   <- throws on a missing f.b

Only f.sbb and f.snb are guarded. So sending a frame without "b" breaks the page.
Two ways out:

  stub B        send a fixed off-screen pose and empty grid deltas. No viewer
                changes, but it draws a phantom second rover.
  guard B       about six small edits so the B panels hide when absent. Cleaner,
                and it is the version worth having.

Also: the viewer's coverage percentage cannot be computed on hardware. coverage()
needs a World for ground truth. mapping.mapped_area_m2(grid) needs only the grid
and is the honest replacement -- area mapped, not fraction of a known truth.

And server.py builds its header from sim.WORLDS[...]() to get grid bounds. On
hardware there is no world; OccupancyGrid grows itself via _grow_to, so bounds
have to come from the grid's own extent instead.


WHAT I WOULD DO FIRST, GIVEN THE CHOICE

Not the full mission. A ten-minute "does the map look like the room" run: push
cuVSLAM pose + stereo points + sonar into OccupancyGrid at 2-3 Hz, publish to the
viewer, and DRIVE IT BY HAND with the existing drive_test moves. No wall-follower,
no frontiers. That tests the whole sensing and display chain -- which is what
"see in real time if the mapping works" actually asks -- and it removes every
autonomy failure mode from the first run. If the map is recognisable, the
follower is worth wiring up. If it is not, no amount of autonomy helps.
